# results/: app-ready exports (prompt 07, Step 6)

Small, tracked tables produced by `python -m src.export` (plus `judgments.csv` from `src/judge/analyze.py`). No embeddings, no full texts, no CORE-derived data. Every text snippet is at most 150 characters (the theory-description excerpts are the only longer text). Three "Personal communication" entries that named individuals (one each in the GWT and panpsychism lists, plus one more panpsychism entry) were removed from `level1_references.csv`; the reference counts in `summary_metrics.csv` are from the full lists. Theory keys: `champagne_semiotic`, `global_workspace`, `integrated_information`, `panpsychism`, `predictive_processing_fep`.

## Read these caveats before using any number

- **Open access only.** The corpus is Europe PMC open-access full text. Coverage skews to neuroscience, psychology and medicine; philosophy-heavy theories (panpsychism, Champagne) are thin.
- **Samples, not totals.** The corpora are designed samples. 1995-2015: 7,794 papers. 2016-2025: 5,438 papers, with the per-theory candidate pool **capped at 400** (cap reached for GWT, IIT, PP; not for Champagne or panpsychism), background capped at 3,000 and random at 1,000 (100 per year, week-clustered). **Mention totals must come from `window_trends.csv` (full Europe PMC server-side counts), never from counting rows in `level2_mentions.csv` or from the 2016-2025 silver-set sizes.**
- **Out-of-time test, not a forecast.** The concept profiles come from current LoC text, which is not limited to papers before 2016. The 2016-2025 evaluation tests whether the frozen method generalizes to later literature.
- **Negatives are unlabeled.** In every AUC, "negatives" are papers that do not name the theory, not known-irrelevant papers, so all AUCs are lower bounds. The **candidate-only AUC is the more informative number**: positives are almost entirely candidate-pool papers, while most negatives are background or random papers.
- **Positives are single-theory papers** (exclusive silver set); many GWT and IIT papers name both theories and are excluded.
- **The judge is an LLM** (Claude Code, blind to method, rank and score). Its verdicts are not ground truth; agreement with the user's hand labels was moderate (kappa 0.44, see `reports/level3_judge.md`).
- **Small n for three theories.** 1995-2015: Champagne n = 3, IIT n = 17, panpsychism n = 7 positives (GWT and PP: test halves, n = 77 and 99). 2016-2025: Champagne 25 (primary subset only 8), IIT 129, panpsychism 61, GWT 213, PP 151. Treat 1995-2015 numbers for Champagne, IIT and panpsychism as qualitative.
- **2024-2025 are incomplete or embargoed in PMC**; read the tail of `window_trends.csv` with care. Strong-phrase hits there are upper bounds on direct mentions (no domain-context rule is possible server-side).

## Files

### `level1_references.csv` (145 rows)
LoC reference lists. `theory`; `raw_reference` (as on the LoC page, clipped to 600 chars); `parsed_title`; `year`; `doi`; `pmcid`; `resolution_status` (`resolved_fulltext`, `resolved_no_fulltext`, `doi_only`, `unresolved`, `non_article`); `oa_fulltext` (`yes` if the full text is in Europe PMC or an open-access PDF was found, the "full-text seed" definition of the Level 1 report); `oa_route` (`europe_pmc`, `oa_pdf`, `none`). OA-PDF seeds are profile-only (not in the corpus).

### `level2_mentions.csv` (2,748 rows)
Papers with a direct, weak or close mention of a theory, in both windows. `theory`; `window` (`1995-2015`, `2016-2025`); `pmcid`; `title`; `year`; `journal`; `mention_tier` (`direct` = strong term plus domain context; `weak` = a term that needs corroboration appeared alone; `close` = close-term only); `mention_strength` (strong-term count in counted sections for direct, the weak or close count otherwise); `first_hit_section` (title, abstract, introduction, body or discussion); `snippet`; **`sample_cap`** and `sample_note` (the cap behind the sample, and a reminder that these rows are a sample and not full totals). Reference lists are never counted as mentions.

### `level3_top.csv` (450 rows)
The top 30 papers per theory in each of three windows (1995-2015, 1995-2020, 1995-2025) under the selected variant V4, with `names_theory_loosely = 0` (papers that name the theory are excluded). `theory`; `window`; `rank`; `pmcid`; `title`; `year`; `score_v4` (z-score based, unitless); `corpus_part`; `best_claim_id` and `best_claim_text` (the theory profile claim closest to the paper's best passage); `snippet` (that passage, clipped to 200 chars); `other_theories_named` (semicolon-separated); **`judged`** (`true`/`false`); `judge_verdict` (R = relevant conceptual neighbor, G = generic consciousness/neuroscience, O = off-topic), `judge_confidence`, `judge_reason`, `judge_evidence` (empty when not judged).
**What `judged = false` means: not judged, NOT relevant and NOT irrelevant.** Only the 1995-2015 V4 top 30 per theory (and the BM25 comparison lists for GWT and PP) were judged. Therefore **every paper published after 2015 is unjudged, and papers in the 1995-2020 and 1995-2025 windows outside the 1995-2015 judged set are unjudged.** Never read an empty verdict as a positive or negative signal. In the 1995-2015 window all 150 rows are judged; in 1995-2020 and 1995-2025, only the carried-over 1995-2015 papers are (73 and 39 rows).

### `window_trends.csv` (155 rows)
Per theory and year 1995-2025: `hits` (open-access full-text papers matching a strong phrase of the theory, Europe PMC server-side count), `total_oa_papers` (all open-access full-text papers that year), `rate_per_10k`. Full counts, not the capped samples.

### `summary_metrics.csv` (one row per theory)
Seed counts (`loc_references`, `loc_fulltext_seeds_any_oa_route`, `loc_seeds_europe_pmc_xml`); **mention totals from the full server-side counts** (`strong_phrase_hits_*_server_side`, `rate_per_10k_*`), never from the capped sample; silver-set sizes (`silver_exclusive_1995_2015_corpus`; `silver_exclusive_2016_2025_capped_sample` with `silver_2016_2025_sample_cap`); V4 and BM25 **candidate-only AUC** with 95% CI for both periods (`V4_cand_auc_1995_2015`, `_ci_lo`, `_ci_hi`, `V4_cand_auc_2016_2025`, ...; same for `BM25_`), with `cand_auc_*_n_positives` and `cand_auc_*_n_candidate_negatives` and `cand_auc_1995_2015_eval_set` (test half for GWT and PP, all exclusive positives otherwise); `judged_R_share_v4_top30_1995_2015` (share of the 30 judged V4 papers rated R) with `judged_n`. CIs are 2,000 bootstrap resamples of the positives (seed 20261011). 1995-2015 and 2016-2025 values come from different negative pools and are indicative, not exactly comparable.

### `discriminability.csv` (100 rows, long format)
Discriminability matrices for V4. `period` (`1995-2015`, `2016-2025`); `pool` (`all_negatives`, `candidate_pool_only`); `variant` (V4); `row_profile` (whose profile scores); `column_positives` (whose masked positives are scored); `n_column_positives`; `mean_percentile_rank` (100 = best, chance 50); `is_diagonal`. The diagonal should be the largest value in each column; it is in every matrix. 2016-2025 uses all years (the 2020-2022-excluded version is in `reports/level3_discriminability_late.md`).

### `judgments.csv` (230 rows)
All LLM judgments (209 unique items plus 21 consistency repeats): `item_id`, `theory`, `pmcid`, `verdict`, `confidence`, `claim_ids`, `reason`, `evidence`, `flags`. Only items from the 1995-2015 V4 and BM25 top-30 lists are judged. `level3_top.csv` joins the original (non-repeat) judgment on theory and pmcid.

# CORE philosophy pilot (separate, exploratory; NOT part of the tables above)

One file, `philosophy_pilot_metrics.csv` (counts and scores only), comes from a small pilot on open-access philosophy and humanities papers from the CORE aggregator for Champagne's semiotic account and panpsychism (`reports/core_pilot.md`, pre-registered in `reports/eval_plan.md`, addenda of 2026-10-14). **Nothing from this pilot enters any table or file above, the app, or the main results.** Every row is labelled `exploratory; source CORE; license unknown`, and `judged` is `false` on every row: no judging has been run, so an empty verdict means unjudged, not relevant or irrelevant. The blind judge is on hold.

## Read these before using any pilot number

- **Exploratory.** The pilot corpus is a designed sample of 2,213 CORE papers (229 term-search candidates, 1,488 adjacent-background papers, 496 distractors that are approximately random within relevance-ordered hits). 1995-2015 is thin: no candidates before 2000.
- **License caveat and unknown license field.** The CORE API returns no license field, so `license` is `unknown` for every record; this does not mean the papers are unrestricted. CORE's terms say its data is for machine processing, does not authorise reproduction or redistribution of the underlying works, and is free for individuals or public research organisations only under stated conditions (a research paper or public report that acknowledges CORE; a licence request for research projects unless the institution is a CORE member). Because of this, no CORE text is tracked anywhere: full texts, chunks, embeddings and judge packets stay in gitignored `data/`, and these files hold only titles, years, repository names, CORE links, DOIs, scores, claim ids, counts and set labels. Follow each paper's own repository terms before reusing anything it links to. CORE should be acknowledged wherever these results are shown.
- **Champagne's full positive set is mostly general Peircean-semiotics papers, not consciousness work.** Of 10 hand-labelled sampled hits, 1 was about consciousness-related work, 8 used Peirce's semiotics outside consciousness (rock art, gender studies, literature, anthropology, physics) and 1 was not a real use. Claims about consciousness-relevant neighbours rest on set B (the consciousness-relevant subset), which has only 18 papers and is exploratory. The full set A (77 papers) mostly measures retrieval of Peircean-semiotics literature in general.
- **Negatives are unlabeled**, so every AUC is a lower bound. The primary metric is AUC against adjacent-background negatives (`negatives = adjacent-background (primary)`); the candidate-pool AUC is the harder comparison. Positives come from the same term search that defines the candidate pool.
- **z-scores and BM25 statistics were computed on the CORE chunk set** (PDF-derived text), not the PubMed Central statistics, so numbers are not exactly comparable with the main results.
- **Near-duplicates were collapsed** in the withheld top-30 lists (one record per cluster, best-ranked kept, lists filled to 30): same normalized title and year, or normalized-title similarity above 0.9. The pilot corpus has 22 such pairs (38 papers; collapsing would remove 20 of 2,213 papers, 0.9%), below the 2% threshold, so the AUC rows are the uncollapsed pre-registered ones. Titles are cleaned of escape sequences and encoding damage.

## `philosophy_pilot_metrics.csv` (63 rows, long format)
`record_type`: `count` (corpus counts by part, chunk count, papers without a reference split, near-duplicate pairs, candidate-pool papers, silver-set sizes with period split), `auc` (masked-positive AUC with 95% bootstrap CI, 2,000 resamples of the positives, seed 20261014) and `auc_year_matched` (sensitivity check: negatives re-weighted to the positives' year distribution, with the effective number of negatives). Columns `positive_set` and `set_label`: A = all exclusive silver papers with a reference split (Champagne 77, panpsychism 89), B = consciousness-relevant subset (18 and 80; Champagne B is below 20, flagged), C = term-precision-trimmed (65 and 83). `negatives`: `adjacent-background (primary)` or `candidate-pool`; `variant`: `V4` or `BM25`; `n_positives`, `n_negatives` beside every AUC. Recall@k and the 2 x 2 discriminability matrix are in `reports/core_pilot.md`.
